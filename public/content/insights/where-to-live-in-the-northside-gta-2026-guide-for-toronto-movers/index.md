If you’re in Toronto, feeling squeezed by space, traffic, and prices, but you’re not ready to disappear into the middle of nowhere, the NorthSide GTA is probably already on your radar — even if you don’t have the language for it yet.

When we say **NorthSide GTA**, we’re talking about the communities just north of the 407 and 401 that still plug into Toronto’s jobs and culture, but give you more house, more community, and more breathing room:

- Uxbridge  
- Stouffville  
- East Gwillimbury (Sharon, Queensville, Holland Landing)  
- Newmarket  
- Aurora  
- Georgina (especially Keswick, Sutton, Pefferlaw)  
- Scugog (Port Perry)  

We live here, we work here, and we help people move here every week. This guide is our honest, **2026-focused breakdown** of where to live in the NorthSide GTA if you’re currently based in Toronto (or the inner suburbs) and trying to figure out which town actually fits your life.

---

## 1. The NorthSide GTA in one sentence

If Toronto is *“maximum access, minimum space,”* the NorthSide GTA flips that: **more space, more community, still close enough to the city that your life doesn’t have to revolve around traffic.**

Most NorthSide GTA buyers come to us with some mix of:

- Wanting a detached home or larger townhome instead of a condo  
- Wanting backyards, parks, and trails for kids and dogs  
- Wanting good schools and safe, family-focused neighbourhoods  
- Wanting less day-to-day chaos, but not wanting to give up access to Toronto entirely  

If that sounds like you, the next question is: **which town?**

---

## 2. How to choose your town (before you fall in love with a listing)

Before we get into specific communities, here’s how we help Toronto movers narrow things down.

### Commute reality

- How many days a week do you actually need to be downtown or in the core?  
- Are you okay with a GO Train ride and a good podcast, or do you need flexible drive times?  

Some towns are GO-first, some are 404-first, some are truly hybrid.

### Budget and home type

- Are you stretching for a forever detached, or are you okay with a townhome if it’s new and walkable?  

In general, you’ll get more **detached options and larger lots** in Uxbridge, Georgina, and parts of Scugog than you will in Aurora or central Newmarket at the same budget.

### Lifestyle and vibe

- Do you want walkable main streets, coffee shops, and local restaurants?  
  - Think Newmarket, Stouffville, Port Perry, Uxbridge.  
- Do you want lake access and boating?  
  - That’s Georgina and Scugog.  
- Do you like quiet, newer subdivisions with parks and schools baked in?  
  - Think East Gwillimbury, newer pockets of Keswick, some Stouffville/Aurora communities.

### Future you (5–10 years out)

- Kids or future kids? You’ll probably care more about school catchments and sports.  
- Planning to work remote long term? Commute becomes less critical; lifestyle and property features move to the top.

We walk every buyer through this framework **before** we ever talk about a specific MLS link. It saves you a lot of weekend driving.

## 3. Town-by-town breakdown

### Uxbridge: Trails, trees, and a real small-town main street

Uxbridge is for people who light up when they hear **“trail system”** and “conservation area.”

Known as the *“Trail Capital of Canada,”* Uxbridge has an extensive network of walking, cycling, and hiking routes, many connecting straight from town into rolling countryside and forest.

The historic core has an actual **main street** — independent restaurants, a brewery, coffee shops, local events — not just big-box plazas.

#### What housing looks like

- Mix of in-town older homes, newer subdivisions, and rural properties just outside the core  
- You’ll see everything from modern family subdivisions to century homes and hobby farms  
- Price-wise, it often gives you **more lot and privacy** than you’d get closer to the 401 corridor at the same budget (with the trade-off being commute time)

#### Commute profile

- Driving to Toronto typically means south via Lakeridge / Brock / 23 to the 401 or 407, then into the city. In light traffic, you can be at the 401 in under an hour; rush hour will push it.  
- Depending on where you work, some buyers also cut across toward **Highway 404** using corridors like Bloomington or Stouffville Road, especially if their job is in the 404/Markham/Richmond Hill/Aurora corridor rather than downtown.  
- Some buyers do a hybrid commute: drive to a GO station (Stouffville or Lincolnville) and train in from there.

#### Best for

- Buyers who care more about nature, trails, and community than shaving 10 minutes off a commute  
- Families who want kids outside more than inside  
- Self-employed / hybrid workers who don’t go downtown five days a week  

---

### Stouffville: Commuter-friendly, still feels like a town

Stouffville is often the first place Toronto movers circle on the map when they want a balance between **commute and lifestyle**.

You get a historic main street, newer neighbourhoods, great parks, and a quietly growing food scene.

The town sits directly on the **Stouffville GO line**, giving you a rail option into Toronto.

#### Commute profile

- **GO Train:** The Stouffville line runs to Union Station, with typical scheduled times of around an hour to an hour and twenty minutes depending on stop pattern and time of day.  
- **Drive:** Quick access to the 404 and 407, making it one of the better drive-commute options in the NorthSide GTA.

#### Housing + lifestyle

- Strong mix of detached homes, townhomes, and some low-rise condo product  
- Walkable pockets close to Main Street for buyers who want to walk to coffee, restaurants, and the GO station  
- Popular with move-up buyers leaving Markham/Richmond Hill who want more house without losing access  

#### Best for

- Buyers who still need regular access to downtown Toronto  
- Families who want organized sports, schools, and parks within a short radius  
- People who like a busy but not overwhelming town feel  

---

### East Gwillimbury (Sharon, Holland Landing, Queensville): New builds and long-term growth

East Gwillimbury is one of the most interesting **growth stories** in the NorthSide GTA.

Large tracts of land in Sharon and Queensville have been earmarked for low-density residential growth, with hundreds of acres planned for future development.

New master-planned communities are bringing brand-new homes, schools, and parks online, with quick access to the 404 and GO (via nearby Newmarket and East Gwillimbury stations).

#### What it feels like

- A mix of brand-new subdivisions and established pockets like Holland Landing  
- You’ll see a lot of young families, strollers, and playgrounds — plus trades vans and work trucks parked in driveways between jobs  

#### Commute profile

- **Driving:** Direct access to Highway 404 makes car commutes into the 401/Don Valley corridor realistic for many buyers.  
- **Transit:** Easy to link into GO service via East Gwillimbury or Newmarket GO for trips to Union Station.

#### Best for

- Buyers who want newer homes with modern layouts (open-concept, larger kitchens, proper mudrooms)  
- Families planning to “grow into” the area as more amenities arrive  
- Toronto condo owners trading up to a detached or larger townhome without going all the way to cottage country  

---

### Newmarket: Full-service town with a real downtown and big-city amenities

Newmarket is the most **“city-like”** of the NorthSide GTA towns while still feeling local.

- A vibrant historic Main Street, with 19th-century architecture and a growing number of restaurants, cafés, and boutiques  
- A regional hub: healthcare (Southlake Regional Health Centre), retail, and services draw people from surrounding communities  

#### Housing + neighbourhoods

- Established neighbourhoods with mature trees and larger lots  
- Newer communities with family-friendly streets, trails, and parks  
- Mix of detached, semi-detached, townhomes, and some condo options  

#### Commute profile

- GO Train & GO Bus options into Toronto  
- Easy access to 404, Davis Drive, and Highway 9 for drivers  

Popular with buyers who want to cut down Toronto time but still want **serious amenities close by.**

#### Best for

- Families who want everything within 10–15 minutes: shopping, healthcare, arenas, fields  
- Buyers who like a walkable downtown plus suburban neighbourhoods  
- Professionals who might work in healthcare, education, or regional offices north of the city  

---

### Aurora: Established, commute-friendly, and school-focused

Aurora feels like the **“established cousin”** in the NorthSide GTA.

- Strong reputation for schools, parks, and organized sports  
- Mature neighbourhoods with tree-lined streets, plus newer developments on the edges  
- A good selection of restaurants, big-box retailers, and local businesses  

#### Commute profile

- Aurora GO Station on the Barrie line, with regular service to Union  
- Quick access to Highway 404 for driving  

Very popular with professionals who split time between downtown and local offices in the 404 corridor.

#### Best for

- Buyers with a strong commute requirement but ready to leave the 416  
- Families prioritizing schools, sports, and stable neighbourhoods  
- Move-up buyers from Markham/Richmond Hill wanting more space with a familiar commute  

---

### Georgina (Keswick, Sutton, Pefferlaw): Lake Simcoe without “cottage country” distance

Georgina is where the NorthSide GTA hits the **Lake Simcoe shoreline.**

The municipality stretches along the southeast shore of Lake Simcoe, about an hour north of Toronto by car in typical conditions.

It’s made up of multiple communities, with **Keswick** as the largest, plus Sutton/Jackson’s Point and Pefferlaw further east.

#### Lifestyle

- Think marinas, beaches, fishing, boating, and winter activities — but with year-round communities, not just cottages  
- Lots of family-oriented subdivisions within a short drive of the water  
- A good fit for people who have always wanted some version of “lake life” without driving two and a half hours every weekend  

#### Commute profile

- Driving via 404 all the way up is the standard; many commuters report ~45–75 minutes to the 401/DVP area depending on time of day and exact location  
- This is usually a driver’s market — there’s no direct GO Train station in Keswick at this point  

#### Best for

- Families who want a lake-oriented lifestyle and don’t mind a drive  
- Remote and hybrid workers who value water, space, and community  
- Buyers who want to grow into a community as more services and growth follow the 404 north  

---

### Scugog (Port Perry): Small historic town, big lake

Scugog centres around **Port Perry**, a classic Ontario small town sitting right on the shore of Lake Scugog.

- Beautiful main street with independent shops, restaurants, and a waterfront park  
- Strong sense of community events, festivals, and local sports  
- A blend of in-town living and rural properties just outside the core  

#### Commute profile

- Most people drive via Highway 12/7 and 407 to reach the GTA job centres  
- This is not a “quick GO ride” town — you choose Scugog because you want the waterfront small-town lifestyle and are okay planning your trips accordingly  

#### Best for

- Buyers who want a true small-town main street plus lake access  
- Families who like community events and slower-paced evenings  
- Hybrid workers who don’t have to be in Toronto every day

## 4. So… where should you live in the NorthSide GTA?

Here’s a simple way to start matching yourself to a town:

**“I want trails, trees, and a real main street.”**  
→ Start with **Uxbridge** and **Port Perry (Scugog).**

**“I need a legit GO Train option into downtown.”**  
→ Look closely at **Stouffville, Aurora, and Newmarket.**

**“I want a new build in a growing family community.”**  
→ Focus on **East Gwillimbury (Sharon / Queensville / Holland Landing)** and newer pockets in **Keswick** and **Stouffville.**

**“I’ve always wanted to live near the water.”**  
→ Shortlist **Georgina (Keswick / Sutton / Pefferlaw)** and **Scugog.**

**“I want big-city amenities but I’m done with Toronto prices and density.”**  
→ Start with **Newmarket** and **Aurora**, then compare against **Stouffville.**
